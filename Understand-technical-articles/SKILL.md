# Understand Technical Articles

## First pass

Read the supplied article before explaining it. If only a link is provided, retrieve the article; if it cannot be accessed, ask for its text rather than guessing its contents. Use the user's stated background when available, without making a background interview a prerequisite.

Give a concise, plain-language first pass under these eight numbered questions. Answer the questions for the reader; do not turn the first pass into a questionnaire.

1. **What problem is it solving?** Identify who faces the problem and why it matters.
2. **What is the main idea?** Explain the central point in two or three simple sentences.
3. **How does it work?** Trace a concrete example from input to output. For a system, show what happens step by step. Label an invented teaching example as illustrative.
4. **What knowledge am I missing?** Identify the terms, concepts, and assumptions needed to follow the article, with brief explanations. Treat these as possible prerequisites, not a claim about what the user knows.
5. **Why was this approach chosen?** Explain the motivation and relevant alternatives. Distinguish the author's stated reasons from your interpretation.
6. **What are the trade-offs and limits?** Discuss relevant gains and sacrifices in speed, cost, complexity, reliability, or security, including conditions where the approach fails or is unsuitable.
7. **What evidence supports the claims?** Identify experiments, benchmarks, examples, or sources and the conditions under which results were obtained. State gaps in the evidence; distinguish the article's claims from independently verified facts.
8. **Where could I apply it?** Connect the idea to a familiar concept or practical problem. Use the user's context when known; otherwise label applications as examples.

Keep prerequisites brief enough to preserve the first pass. Cite the article where useful, and clearly distinguish any supplementary sources from it. If the article does not answer a question, say so rather than inventing details.

## Deeper follow-up

After the first pass, when the user asks to go deeper without specifying a focus, ask which numbered question or topic they want to explore next. Offer the eight labels as a compact menu and wait for their choice before starting the next phase. Do not automatically expand all eight sections. If they already name a focus, use it without asking them to select it again.

If they choose missing knowledge (question 4), ask which particular technology or concept they want to learn, including an option such as “I'm not sure.” Use the article's actual prerequisites as suggested choices. If they already name a concept, proceed directly.

If the user does not know which prerequisite to choose, suggest a short list of specific concepts from the article, explain briefly what each would unlock, and recommend the most foundational starting point. Ask them to select one or accept the recommendation before teaching it; do not ask them to name a concept they have just said they cannot identify. If they explicitly ask you to choose and proceed, do so.

Teach the selected focus at the requested depth with a concrete example, then connect it back to the relevant part of the article. Keep the next phase scoped to that focus. When the user requests another unspecified deeper pass, repeat the focus-selection step using the current learning context.
